Indian Market Observatory

Quantitative Investing Through Behavioral Finance — an educational quantitative research backtest, not investment advice and not a prediction of future performance.

Research question: can simple systematic strategies based on momentum and mean reversion outperform passive investing in Indian large-cap equities? This project tests whether patterns discussed in behavioral finance translate into simple, mechanical quantitative signals.

Mean reversion here ranks on raw prior-month return only — it is not a z-score, moving-average, or Bollinger-band model.

Mᵢ,ₜ = (Pᵢ,ₜ₋₁ − Pᵢ,ₜ₋₂) / Pᵢ,ₜ₋₂

Signal: each stock's own return over the prior month.

Rₚ,ₜ = 0.5·R₁,ₜ + 0.5·R₂,ₜ

Portfolio return: equal-weighted average of the two selected stocks' returns this month.

Vₜ = Vₜ₋₁ · (1 + Rₚ,ₜ)

Portfolio value compounds month over month from ₹100,000.

DDₜ = (Vₜ − RunningMaxₜ) / RunningMaxₜ

Drawdown: distance below the highest value reached so far.

Transaction Cost Sensitivity

Round-trip cost applied only to the active strategies on rebalance months. No cost assumption here is asserted as "realistic" — these are illustrative stress points.

Concentration

Both strategies hold 2 of 5 stocks each month — a concentrated construction. This concentration may contribute to the higher volatility and drawdowns observed in the active strategies relative to the more diversified benchmark; causality was not experimentally isolated.

Benchmark Limitation

The Nifty 50 Price Index excludes dividends. A Nifty 50 Total Return Index comparison would be a natural future extension, not something substituted here.

Behavioral Finance Framework

These are theoretical interpretations, not causal findings established by this backtest. The results do not establish that investor psychology caused the observed returns.

Momentum — possible behavioral explanations

  • One possible explanation is investor underreaction to new information.
  • Gradual diffusion of information across the market.
  • Herding behavior among investors.
  • Confirmation bias reinforcing an existing trend.

Mean Reversion — possible behavioral explanations

  • The strategy is conceptually consistent with investor overreaction.
  • Loss aversion driving panic selling.
  • Anchoring to recent price levels.
  • Temporary mispricing that partially corrects.

What Do the Results Suggest?

This is not an investment recommendation. Over this particular sample, the Nifty 50 Price Index accumulated more value than either active strategy. Momentum finished above its starting capital; Mean Reversion finished approximately flat. The results therefore do not demonstrate that these simple behavioral strategies outperformed passive investing during the sample. This does not establish that momentum or mean reversion never work — only that they did not in this specific window, universe, and cost assumption.

Limitations

  • Small sample — only five stocks.
  • Short time period — approximately three years.
  • Concentration — only two stocks held at a time.
  • Baseline assumes zero transaction costs.
  • Baseline assumes zero slippage.
  • Benchmark excludes dividends (price index, not TRI).
  • No direct investor-level psychological data was measured.
  • Historical performance does not establish future performance.
  • No out-of-sample validation on a separate unseen period.
  • Only one-month return ranking was tested — parameter simplicity.

Future Research

Broader universes (Nifty 100/500); longer lookback windows; alternative mean-reversion definitions; volatility filters; sector-neutral construction; modeled transaction costs; a dividend-adjusted (TRI) benchmark; out-of-sample and walk-forward testing; regime analysis; factor models; machine-learning ranking; and direct behavioral/sentiment data. All labeled as future work, not results of this project.

Reproducibility & Data Integrity

  • Data source: Yahoo Finance via yfinance
  • Tickers: RELIANCE.NS, TCS.NS, HDFCBANK.NS, INFY.NS, ICICIBANK.NS
  • Benchmark: ^NSEI, monthly, Aug 2023 – Jun 2026
  • Selection: top 2 / bottom 2 by previous-month return, 50/50, monthly
  • Baseline: 0 bps costs, 0% risk-free rate
  • Validated: no missing values, no duplicate dates, complete monthly coverage, chronological order.
  • Zero look-ahead mismatches: each month's signal independently re-derived as equal to the prior month's realized return.
  • Exactly two securities selected every active month; weights summed to 100%.
  • No missing return was silently treated as zero.
  • Benchmark file: 35 monthly observations, no missing values or duplicate dates.
